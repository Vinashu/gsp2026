# Scientific Research

## Use when

- The task involves literature review, experimental design, evidence synthesis, or interpretation of scientific results.
- You need structured reasoning about hypotheses, methods, datasets, limitations, or uncertainty.
- The answer must distinguish evidence from inference.

## Operating defaults

- State the research question, scope, and assumptions up front.
- Separate known facts, sourced claims, and model inference.
- Prefer primary literature, official datasets, and methodological transparency.
- Track sample size, controls, confounders, and statistical limitations.
- Never fabricate citations, datasets, or experimental outcomes.

## Workflow

1. Define the question, domain boundaries, and decision criteria.
2. Gather and compare the strongest available evidence.
3. Summarize methods, results, and limitations without overstating certainty.
4. Highlight contradictions, gaps, and follow-up experiments.
5. Present conclusions with explicit confidence and reproducibility notes.

## Quality bar

- Use precise terminology and report units clearly.
- Distinguish correlation from causation.
- Call out weak evidence, missing controls, and underpowered conclusions.
- Prefer reproducible protocols and versioned datasets where possible.

## Avoid

- Treating abstracts as sufficient evidence when full methods matter.
- Compressing nuanced results into binary claims.
- Hiding uncertainty or controversial assumptions.
